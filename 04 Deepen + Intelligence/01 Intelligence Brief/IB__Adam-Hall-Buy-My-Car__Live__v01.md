## Snapshot – one-line commercial read

The constraint is not demand or trust; it is that the entire conversion system lives in one person's head, which makes the business unhireable-into, capacity-capped and unsellable.

## Current State (Confirmed)

Adam Hall Buy My Car turns over approximately $5M a year. It buys around 25 cars a month (range 20–30), split roughly 90% retail and 10% wholesale, at an average retail gross of about $4,200 per car. Customers are mostly 60–75 and older – downsizers, licence-loss sellers, and estate or health-driven sellers – who choose this business on trust first and price second.

This is a trust-conversion business operating inside a car-buying shell, not a car business that happens to be trusted. The near-term target is $7M, with a longer 10x path toward a named, trusted, sellable buying brand. The north star is Cars For Us: founded 2019, grew to $500M, sold to Toyota for approximately $120M.

## The Constraint

Adam is the system. Valuation logic, yes/no instinct, customer-trust technique and payment/settlement discipline live only in his head, undocumented. Every transaction depends on Adam personally reading the seller, setting the number and calling the decision. Nothing about how he does this is written down, tested or transferable.

Empathy first then the solution. Daily self-criticism. Ownership not excuses. Stay in the room. Value your time commercially. The business hires and trains for these values first, skills second. George is the cautionary case of what happens when that order reverses – skill hired before values.

## Brand Identity – open departure (scroll motion)

Brand Identity Edition 1 rules out scroll-driven motion in absolute terms: "anything on scroll: never — no parallax, no fade-up-on-scroll, no counters triggering in the viewport". The marketplace build complied with that throughout, which is why its Reveal component renders its children statically rather than animating them in.

On 20 August Adam asked for the carmarketplace.com.au home page to open on a film that moves frame by frame as the reader scrolls. It is built and shipped on the branch preview. This is a deliberate exception, not an oversight.

Two more followed on 21 August, both scroll-driven and both on the same footing: the header now hides as you scroll down and returns as you scroll up, and it reads the ground beneath it and inverts, running white over the films, the green bands and the footer and reverting to cream over the pale pages. The header's own stylesheet previously recorded the opposite position in writing, "always visible (no scroll hide/show, no tone logic)", so this reverses a decision that was made deliberately the first time.

Two things follow from that:

- Adam is taking the treatment to Liz so Edition 1 can be amended rather than quietly contradicted. Until she rules, the identity document and the built site disagree on this one point, and the site is the departure.
- If the answer is no, the reversal is contained: delete `marketplace/components/site/ScrollFilm.tsx` and its stylesheet and restore the `mp2-hero` block in `marketplace/app/page.tsx`. The CSS for that block has been kept in place for exactly this reason.

The treatment itself holds the rest of the identity: the durations are the identity's own steps, nothing plays on its own, and readers on reduced motion or save-data get a single still frame with no pinning and no film downloaded at all.
